The poem

Stanza 1lines 1–8

  1. If you sit down at set of sun
  2. And count the acts that you have done,
  3. And, counting, find
  4. One self-denying deed, one word
  5. That eased the heart of him who heard,
  6. One glance most kind
  7. That fell like sunshine where it went –
  8. Then you may count that day well spent.

Stanza 2lines 9–16

  1. But if, through all the livelong day,
  2. You’ve cheered no heart, by yea or nay –
  3. If, through it all
  4. You’ve nothing done that you can trace
  5. That brought the sunshine to one face –
  6. No act most small
  7. That helped some soul and nothing cost –
  8. Then count that day as worse than lost.

    In the poem, the speaker says that if we do nothing kind all day, the day is “worse than lost.” I think this is because a kind act is easy and “nothing cost.” We had a chance to bring “sunshine to one face,” but we didn’t use it. When we lose something, it is an accident. A day with no kindness is our choice.
